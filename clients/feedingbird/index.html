Three concepts, and the one I would run.

Alex Lamb · August 2026 · for Tasha Willingham

All three come from the same place: the top reason people give for having no estate documents is not cost and not fear. It is "I do not think I have enough assets to need one," at twenty seven percent in Trust and Will's 2026 report. Cost is fifteen. Complexity twelve. Discomfort twelve.

So the three big blockers are self-exclusion, inertia, and disbelief that it is quick. Each concept below attacks one of them. They are not variations on a theme, they are three different arguments, and they produce differently.

Concept one · recommended

It Counts.

Name one specific thing someone already owns, and confirm it counts as an estate. The truck. The lake place. The ring. The dog.

Attacks: "I do not have enough." 27%, the largest block.
Produces as: type cards, thirty second films, a printable list.

Concept two

Already Decided.

You have made these calls in your head years ago. You know who gets the house. Nobody else does. Saying it out loud is the whole job.

Attacks: inertia. "Have not got round to it" 23% plus "do not know where to start" 17%.
Produces as: two-hander films, couples and siblings, real conversations.

Concept three

Thirty Minutes.

Show the thirty minutes instead of claiming them. Film one person doing the entire thing, start to finish, clock running in the corner.

Attacks: disbelief in the core claim. Cheapest to produce of the three.
Produces as: one long take, cut into many short proofs.

Why I would run It Counts

It is the only one of the three that grows the market instead of competing for it. Already Decided and Thirty Minutes both talk to people who have accepted they need this and have not acted. That is the smaller half. It Counts talks to the twenty seven percent who have quietly decided the category is not for them, and nobody in the category is speaking to them at all.

At 50+ that self-assessment is almost always wrong, which is what makes it worth attacking. Someone who says "I do not have an estate" usually owns the largest asset of their life, plus the two things families actually fight over: property with meaning attached, and an unwritten assumption about who gets it. A house with no plan behind it is usually headed to probate, and eighteen months of that lands on somebody they love.

It fits the voice without editing. Two words, declarative, no hedge, no plea, in the brand's own register: short, certain, no hedge. It never reaches for mortality or pressure, so it clears the guardrails by construction rather than by review. The product's core claims ride in the close of every piece, never the hook.

The other two do not go in a drawer. Already Decided is the natural second wave once It Counts has done the qualifying, and Thirty Minutes is the cheapest thing on the list to shoot, so it is the one I would put in front of anyone who clicks and does not convert.

The reference behind all five

What actually happens to each thing with no will. This is the source the reels are written from, and the detail frames pull from it.

What you ownWith no will, in plain terms
Your houseIt goes through probate before anyone inherits. State law sets the shares, not you. Probate runs about eighteen months on average, and the people you love are the ones sitting in it.
A retirement account or life insuranceThe beneficiary form decides, and a will cannot override it. If it names an ex spouse, or nobody, that is what stands. Most people have never reread the form they filled in on their first day at a job.
Jewelry, tools, photographs, anything with no titleNo record of intent exists, so it comes down to what family members remember you saying. This is where most estates actually turn into arguments, and it is the cheapest thing in the world to prevent.
Land or a share of a family propertyEach generation with no plan adds more part owners. Eventually nobody can sell, refinance or repair it without tracking down everyone, and the property quietly becomes unusable.
Digital accounts and photosEach service decides. Some memorialize, some close the account, some will not grant access to family at all without documented authorization. Fifteen years of photographs can end.
Your petsIn law they are property. Nobody is obliged to take them, and the choice falls to whoever is standing there.
A small businessWhat happens depends on the structure and any agreement already in place. Without either, staff and clients can be waiting weeks for somebody to have the authority to act.
An account nobody knows aboutAfter a period of no contact it can be turned over to the state as unclaimed property. It is recoverable, but only by someone who knows to look.
Before any of this runs, your legal team signs off on the wording, state by state. These are the general rules and they vary, particularly on intestacy shares and on digital access. I have written them as plain language a person can act on, not as legal advice, and the beneficiary designation point is the one I would want checked most carefully because it is also the most useful thing on the page.

Why this pairing is the engine of the whole campaign. The hook gets the view. The consequence frame gets the share, because it is genuinely new information rather than a warning. And the consequence is always specific to something the person has already ticked, so it never reads as a scare. It reads as somebody finally explaining the part nobody explains.

It also gives you the same asset three ways: a carousel, a set of thirty second films where one person reads one line of the table, and the printable page that holds all five at once.

A note

On the illustration

Type-only is a decision, not an oversight.

The hand-drawn style is the most distinctive thing the brand owns and it is also the one asset that does not survive automation. It cannot be generated reliably, so anything built on it moves at the speed of one pair of hands. That is the right trade in some places and the wrong one in a weekly series.

So I would split the two. Illustration stays where it earns its cost and where people spend real time: the product, onboarding, the site, the moments that carry the warmth. Social runs on type and on real footage, which are the two things that can be produced at volume and on brand without a queue.

Where illustration does belong in this campaign is the printable list, which is one asset, used for months, and worth an illustrator's week.

The team

Cadence

Three weeks, launch relative

One thing up front, because it changes the shape of everything below. I looked at the accounts. Instagram is at thirty one followers with nothing posted, Facebook at twenty nine. That is a standing start rather than a slow channel, and the two call for opposite plans. So paid carries distribution from day one, and organic does a different job: the library, and the proof the brand exists when somebody checks.

Week one · let cold traffic vote Three cards and one film in paid, against a broad 50+ homeowner audience. The organic grid gets the same pieces, not because it will reach anyone yet, but because an empty profile is a reason not to buy. The read at the end of the week is share rate and cost per click by item.
Week two · widen behind the winner, start the list Two more films built around whichever object won, plus the first real customer telling their own version. The list goes live and becomes the primary paid destination.
Week three · answer the legitimacy question The objection that stops purchases rather than attention. Attorney-reviewed, state-specific, valid where you live, said in the It Counts voice rather than as a trust badge nobody reads. One walkthrough film showing the actual thirty minutes, which is Concept Three earning its keep. Retarget everyone who took the list and did not start a plan.

What this buys beyond the three weeks. Eight to twelve pieces of tested creative and a first read on which objects travel, which is the input for everything after. Follower count is not the goal in this window and I would not report it as one.

What I would watch instead of impressions: cost per click by item, share rate on paid, list downloads, and the percentage of downloads that start a plan within seven days. The last one is the only number that separates an idea that sells from an idea that merely travels.

How it keeps going

The pipeline behind it

A campaign is one output. The thing worth building together is the loop that produces the next one, and the one after that.

MondayPull what ran. Rank by cost per click and share rate, not by what either of us liked.
ConceptsThree to five new items or angles off the winners, written as finished copy, not as briefs.
You pickYou see them as cards, not as descriptions. Yes, no, or change one line.
ProductionCards same week. Films batched to one shoot day a month.
Back to MondayEvery round starts with the last round's numbers, so the library compounds instead of resetting.

The point of the loop is that you are never handed a blank page. You get finished work to react to, and a yes, a no, or one line changed is all it takes from you. One shoot day a month plus a weekly card batch is roughly thirty pieces, which is enough to run paid and organic on both segments without anyone waiting on anyone.

Where I let AI do the work

  • Resizing and reframing one film across every placement
  • Finding the moments in a podcast, then a person picks
  • Subtitles, cleanup, background removal, audio leveling
  • First draft copy variants for testing, twenty headlines to choose three
  • The fifty state permutations, from a real source, checked by a person
  • Pulling and ranking competitor ads and reviews, which is how the tables above got made
  • Mood boards and look development before a shoot

Where I would not, and why

  • Any person speaking about their own family. That is the single fastest way to lose a fifty plus audience on a trust product
  • Testimonials or anything implying a real customer
  • The hand drawn illustration. It cannot hold the style, and near misses read worse than not trying
  • Legal specifics of any kind without a human check
  • Final copy. Drafts yes, shipped words no

The rule I work to: the tools carry the volume, a person carries anything a viewer is asked to believe. On a product where the entire sale is trust, that line is not a preference, it is the strategy.
