The Legal 500 US: what it is, how it runs, and how it lines up with Chambers USA

The 2026 edition ranked more firms and named more lawyers than the 2025 edition. Its research calendar sits close to the Chambers USA calendar, and the structure changes for 2027.

October 16, 2026 · Alex Lamb

Quick answer

The Legal 500 US is a guide that ranks US law firms by practice area and recognises individual lawyers, and its 2026 rankings went live June 10, 2026, covering 126 practice areas. Chambers USA 2026 was published June 4, 2026, 6 days earlier, so both guides now publish in June and both ask for submissions in the late months of the year.

What the Legal 500 US is

The Legal 500 US is a guide that ranks US law firms by practice area and names individual lawyers within them. Legal 500 announced in a press release on Newsfile that the United States 2026 Nationwide rankings went live June 10, 2026.

The 2026 edition covers 126 practice areas. Lawyers are recognised under 4 labels in the release: Hall of Fame, Leading Partners, Next Generation Partners and Leading Associates.

Tier One Rankings wrote that the guide has broadened its scope to recognise mid-market firms and specialist boutiques, not just top-tier work. That matters for firms that have never thought of themselves as Legal 500 candidates.

How big the 2026 edition was

Every figure in this section comes from the Legal 500 press release. These are the publisher's own counts. I found no independent count in the sources.

Legal 500 reported over 440 ranked firms, a 39% increase from 2025. It recognised 3,659 lawyers, up from 3,423 in 2025. The split: 647 Hall of Fame lawyers, 2,126 Leading Partners, 736 Next Generation Partners and 228 Leading Associates.

Leading Partners is the largest group. Leading Associates is the smallest. For a firm deciding which lawyers to put forward, that shows where most of the recognition sits.

The edition also added new categories for boutique firms in white collar crime and commercial disputes. A boutique in either area now has a category built for it.

How the research cycle runs

I took the 2026 timeline from Tier One Rankings. Client referees and editorial submissions were both due November 14, 2025. Firm interviews ran December 2025 to February 2026. The guide published in June 2026.

The structure changes next. Starting with the 2027 guides, Legal 500 organises US research into Nationwide, State and US Elite, according to its press release. Nationwide covers firms with national reach and multi-state capability. State covers firms with strong statewide presence and market visibility. US Elite covers boutique and specialist firms with concentrated geographic or practice-specific reputations.

The sources here do not give the 2027 Legal 500 deadlines. Check them with Legal 500 before planning the submission calendar.

Legal 500 vs Chambers USA: the timeline

Chambers USA 2026 was published June 4, 2026, according to a Porter Wright press release. That was 6 days before the Legal 500 US 2026 release. Porter Wright reported 22 attorneys and 10 firm practices ranked.

The ranking formats differ. Chambers and Partners says it ranks firms and lawyers in bands from 1, the highest, to 6, the lowest. The Legal 500 press release describes its lawyer recognition through the Hall of Fame, Leading Partners, Next Generation Partners and Leading Associates labels.

The calendars overlap. National Law Review reported that research for the Chambers USA 2027 guide began in August 2026, with deadlines running to December 3, 2026. Legal 500's deadline for the 2026 guide was November 14, 2025. A team that submits to both should expect the work for each to land in the same autumn months.

What to weigh before committing

Track first. Under the 2027 structure, a firm has to know whether it reads as Nationwide, State or US Elite. That choice shapes which peers it is measured against.

Then fit. A boutique in white collar crime or commercial disputes now has a category designed for it. A mid-market firm sits inside the broader scope Tier One Rankings described.

Then capacity. Referees, submissions and interviews for both guides fall in the same stretch of the year. The question is whether the team can do both well, or should put its effort into one.
